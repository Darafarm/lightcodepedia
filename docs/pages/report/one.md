## The Student with the Messy Notes

A student had an important exam coming up. She spent hours typing her study notes into a document. Everything was written as one long block of text.

It looked something like this:

"Today we learned about ancient civilizations. Egypt developed along the Nile River. The pyramids were built as tombs. Greece introduced democracy. Rome built one of the greatest empires in history..."

When she tried to study, she couldn't quickly find the section she needed. Every topic blended into the next. She became frustrated and wasted time searching for information.

Her teacher looked at the notes and asked.

> "Do you want to organise and restructure your notes to be understandable by you and all? You need to use appropriate **headers** for clarity"

A **header** is a title or heading that introduces a new section of a document and are set according to the level.

Below is an example of headers (Level 1-4) in markdown usually depicted with a hash(#) key. Headers can be from level 1 - 6 and the level would generally be reflected by the corresponding number of **Hashes(#)**

# Header Level 1

## Header Level 2

### Header Level 3

#### Header Level 4

##### Header Level 5

###### Header Level 6



# Best Practices

✅ Use only one `#` heading as the page title.

✅ Organize content logically.

✅ Don't skip levels unnecessarily.
